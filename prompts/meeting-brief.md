# Meeting Brief

Source: $ARGUMENTS

In 30 minutes, I have a meeting about this source. It holds a lot of data, and it overwhelms me. Give me its essence: the few elements that truly matter.

## Read the source

- URL: fetch it. Email: find and read the full thread. File path: read the whole file. Plain text: use it as is.
- Read all of it before you write. Do not summarize from a partial read.
- If you cannot get the source, stop and tell me. Do not guess its content.

## Output format

### The One Thing
One sentence: the single most important message of the source. If I remember nothing else, I remember this.

### The Trinity
The next three key points, one line each. They can follow from The One Thing, or stand apart. Pick what changes a decision, not what fills space.

### Dig In (3-5 questions)
Three to five questions to ask in the meeting. Each question presses on a pivotal point. Ask for:
- **Clarification**: what exactly does this mean, with numbers or examples?
- **Simplification**: what is the simplest version of this, and what can we remove?
- **Practicality**: what do we do next, who owns it, and how do we know it works?

Ask like Steve Jobs or Elon Musk would: cut through vague claims and jargon to the essence. Challenge the assumptions. Ask for first principles and evidence.

## Rules

- Be brief. The full brief takes under 2 minutes to read.
- Use the words of the source for key terms, so I recognize them in the meeting.
- Show facts the source states. Mark your own inferences as such.
- No preamble, no closing summary.
